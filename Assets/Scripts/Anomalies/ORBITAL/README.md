# ORBITAL

Open `Assets/Scenes/S-6_ORBITAL.unity`, or select **ORBITAL / STAGE_005** in the level carousel. The stage is enabled in Build Settings. Press Play to start the normal roster, countdown and match flow.

## Behavior

- A 23,000-particle cut face fills the center of the XZ arena. The entire circular cross section faces the overhead camera, with a faint far hemisphere behind it. There is no removed quadrant. Individual points blink in and out at new independently sampled positions every 0.09–0.32 seconds; they do not travel along hidden orbits.
- The original f/d mixture is followed by **(3,0,0), (3,2,2), (4,1,1), (4,2,0), (4,2,1)**, then repeats. Transitions start every **15 gameplay seconds** and redistribute blinking samples smoothly over **2 seconds**. Both timings and the starting formation are exposed under **Orbital Cloud Visual / Formation cycle** on `GameplayObjects / ORBITAL Cloud`. The transition duration is included in the interval; pause/countdown/closed match clock/anomalies also pause the formation clock.
- The new formations preserve radial nodes and squared spherical-harmonic angular structure. Radial probability weighting, contrast compression and common arena-scale bounds keep the shells readable and playable. These are orbital-inspired game distributions, not calibrated atomic wavefunctions. Violet tails are low probability; amber/yellow lobes are higher probability. The mathematical shape basis follows the radial/angular separation described in [MIT's hydrogen-atom notes](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/18-755-lie-groups-and-lie-algebras-ii-spring-2024/mit18_755_s24_lec12.pdf).
- Particles and strikes share `OrbitalDensity.Evaluate`. Every eligible body gets one independent encounter trial per physics step, regardless of its number of colliders. The hit probability for density `d`, peak encounter rate `lambda`, and step `dt` is `1 - exp(-lambda*d*dt)`. Default `lambda = 2.4/s`: stationary exposure at peak density gives about 91% chance of at least one hit in one second, versus about 21% at 10% density. The trial samples the incoming contact face of the body's collider.
- During transitions, both gameplay and display use the same smooth blend of source/target densities. The samplers account for each formation's integrated weight when mixing samples. All six sampling tables are prepared at build time; switches reuse the existing particle pool. Points continue to blink independently rather than tracing paths between formations.
- Direction and handedness are freshly randomized for each trial. A confirmed strike applies a planar velocity impulse, flashes the contact, and displays an ellipse around the cloud center plus a stemless direction arrowhead. The script defaults to 0.34 seconds; the scene's current 0.5-second setting is preserved. All three effects keep alpha at one: geometry grows and shrinks, with ring thickness going from zero to full to zero. The orbit and arrow retain a subtle additive RGB glow without global bloom or alpha fading. The ellipse passes through the contact and its tangent agrees with the sampled impulse. It is a brief impact visualization, not an electron trajectory simulation.
- Every accepted strike ejects one **Mass Nugglet** in an independent random planar direction. This prefab variant is one third the visual size of `Orbital Mass Nugget` and grants one tenth of its three-Grow reward through the existing `GrowScaled` API. Nuggets restore mass; a pooled `+X% MASS` toast reports the actual amount restored, including partial pickups at the mass cap. Standard nuggets retain their full reward. Nugglets keep the 0.3-second pickup grace, low drag and 18-second lifetime. A 64-slot pool reuses inactive pickups, shrinking an old pickup away before recycling an occupied slot. Impacts use 24 reusable slots.
- All nugget types share particle-size entrances (0.18 seconds) and exits (0.16 seconds). Collection disables colliders immediately to prevent duplicate rewards, then completes the visual exit. The shared frictionless pickup material and planar collision response reflect wall-normal velocity with restitution 0.85, preserve tangent velocity, and work below Unity's bounce threshold without adding a minimum kick.
- The gameplay cloud enters over 1.4 seconds: every sample begins at size zero and the darkest purple, then smoothly reaches its normal size and density color. Cloud particles now depth-test against opaque surfaces, including black actor materials. Menu and instructions previews keep their existing entrance behavior.
- Players, uncaptured gameplay amplifier cores and live dynamic enemies are eligible. Presentation cores, weapons, sensors, dead/paused actors, nuggets and unrelated objects are excluded. The level's real drone prefab was exercised. Nuggets cannot cause recursive electron strikes.
- Countdown, closed scoring, paused match clock, time pause, anomalies and a disabled cloud stop strikes. Player recoil uses the existing external-stun and action-momentum hooks for a brief 0.1-second reaction. Strikes do not directly deduct health or award combat score.

## Integration and tuning

The scene uses the DYNAMO prototype foundation: camera, arena bounds and grid, goals, players and roster, Rewired, match timing, score service and HUD, power-up spawning, audio, death/end flow, and the drone director. DYNAMO storms and gallery demonstrations are removed. A level-specific empty StageProfile prevents unrelated timed anomaly minigames. Match length and score economy continue to come from the shared profile (currently 1,200 seconds).

`Amplifier + Resonance — Spawn Cycle` now uses the existing DYNAMO coordinator, `345 Hz Particle Encounter` pattern, and `Amplifier Core - Spawn Cycle` prefab. It waits 6 seconds after scoring opens, forms the pattern, places one core safely in neutral territory, then lets the existing goal/capture system raise the team's amplifier multiplier. Capture retires the pattern and absorbs the core before a randomized 6–10-second respawn delay. The next pair runs alongside the cloud's independent formation cycle. Core spawn/capture animations remain immune to electron strikes. The score service and HUD retain ownership of team multipliers; cloud strikes do not award amplifier tiers. The coordinator has explicit ORBITAL bounds/score references, and the disabled drone director references its active pattern for safe placement if later enabled. Old standalone gameplay cores remain inactive to prevent duplicate spawning; presentation HUD cores are preserved.

`GameplayObjects / ORBITAL Cloud` exposes cloud radius, count, lifetime, point size and opacity; peak strike rate, recoil and speed cap; flash lifetime; and nugget speed, lifetime and capacity. Cloud data and gameplay remain separate so particle quality does not alter hit probabilities. The reusable prefab intentionally requires scene score/anomaly references when used with `waitForMatch` enabled.

`outerTailExtension = 0.25` extends only the gameplay cloud's sparse outer taper: its support grows from 4.9 to 6.125 world units. All probabilities inside 78% of the original radius, including the dense lobes, remain unchanged. The display sampler, bounds and hazard use the same extended density. Raising the sample count from 20,000 to 23,000 preserves approximately the previous inner particle concentration. The level-select diorama and instructions preview retain their existing extent.

New assets also include an animated level-select icon, an instructions panel, the ORBITAL level definition, particle/impact materials and shaders, and pooled pickup prefabs. Shared integration includes `PlayerControllerScript.GrowScaled`, the standard Matter nugget prefab/lifecycle, and the existing toast pool's mass-reward display. Package versions, physics layers and input bindings are unchanged. The existing LevelCatalog receives one entry; EditorBuildSettings receives one scene appended to preserve prior build indices.

The level-select icon uses `fullVolume`: 7,000 blinking samples throughout the complete 3D density, with a tilted, slowly turning presentation. Sampling includes cylindrical volume weighting so the interior is volumetric rather than a stack of cut faces. Gameplay and the instructions preview continue to use the cut-face mode.

## Development checks

- `MASSIVE > ORBITAL > Create missing level assets`: creates missing assets and registers the level, preserving an existing ORBITAL scene.
- `MASSIVE > ORBITAL > Validate probability and scene`: run with ORBITAL open. Checks density bounds/symmetry, peak and support, 20,000 sampled positions/directions, handedness, timestep invariance, shader import, particles, scene references, one listener, catalog and Build Settings.
- `MASSIVE > ORBITAL > Run live interaction checks`: run in Play Mode in ORBITAL. Exercises actual player, core and drone bodies; recoil; one flash/nugget per strike; pickup, expiry, pause/closed scoring, outside-cloud immunity, bounded populations and disable/re-enable cleanup. Temporary actors/settings are restored; do not save runtime state. The runner is editor-only.
- `MASSIVE > ORBITAL > Run live polish checks`: checks real arena bounces at slow and fast oblique velocities for all three pickup prefabs; size entrances/exits, expiry, capped mass/toast, duplicate protection, reuse, cloud entrance and fully opaque impact lifetime. Saves native Game view screenshots. Run the interaction checks first in a fresh Play session, since polish checks deliberately add a preview flash outside gameplay strike accounting.
- `MASSIVE > ORBITAL > Validate formation cycle and amplifier wiring`: checks six-state order/wrap, exact interval behavior, normalized densities, radial/angular nodes, sampling concentration, source spawn settings, goals and scene references.
- `MASSIVE > ORBITAL > Run formation and amplifier overlap checks`: use a fresh ORBITAL Play session. Captures all six states with temporarily accelerated timing, exercises actual managed-core recoil/capture, duplicate protection, multiplied score awards, HUD updates and delayed respawn while formations keep cycling. Timing/rate changes are restored; the test's score/capture events are runtime-only. Stop Play afterward without saving runtime state.
