# Dynamo Amplifier treatment workbench

Select **VectorGridGPU**, then click **Open Live Preview — no Play Mode needed** in its Amplifier Goal Treatments Inspector. The same window is under **MASSIVE > Amplifier Treatments**. **Preview running / Preview paused**, **Capture now**, and **Loop Capture** are at the top; capture and loop are also fixed at the bottom while the settings scroll. Choose ×2/×4/×8 to see held activity; ×1 is neutral between captures. Loop interval uses the presentation clock, so playback speed also changes the repeat timing. Save the scene to keep edits made outside Play Mode; Play Mode tweaks are temporary.

Four starting combinations:

1. **Restrained / dispersive:** narrow broken fringe, two opposed packets, subtle held line and travelling wave.
2. **Cloud / nodal packets:** finer density-based grains, broader falloff, stronger gaps, occasional small held packets.
3. **Polar / living filaments:** alternating poles, fine branching, variable lifetimes and occasional reconnection loops.
4. **Curling field discharge:** cloud plus sparse branching, with a radial/tangential quadrature displacement through the existing grid.

Click **Capture** repeatedly; **Hold ×1 / ×2 / ×4 / ×8** sets a visual preview multiplier. Light/Dark selects the receiving goal. Preview never changes match scoring. Disable Preview to follow MatchScoreService. Actual core capture is connected through AmplifierGoalCapture; the existing physical capture and scoring authority are retained. Legacy capture feedback is independently available, off by default in this scene to allow clean comparisons.

## Controls and interpretation

- **Allow Effects Over Grid**, in the fixed preview controls and Inspector's Effect Containment section, controls spill onto the playing field. Off clips cloud, flare, atmosphere, branching and Color Interference together at the moving boundary; their individual settings are retained. It also suppresses the optional field discharge. Capture/held boundary-line treatments keep their own controls. The mask uses the same inverse displacement map as the goal, rather than a stationary vertical cutoff. On restores spill and the configured discharge.
- **Interference Shimmer**, under Color Interference, provides the same Enabled, Scale, Contrast, Curl and Drift Speed controls for its fine spectral pattern. Off or zero contrast retains a smooth localized color layer. Zero drift freezes only the fine pattern; the broader clustered/drifting mode envelope remains controlled by Interference Drift and Beat Frequency. Defaults preserve the earlier pattern.
- **Cloud Shimmer / Flare Shimmer / Wisp Shimmer** appear expanded beside their layer controls in both the Inspector and Live Preview. Each has Enabled, Scale (larger = wider spacing), Contrast, Curl, and Drift Speed (zero = no pattern drift, negative = reverse). Off or zero contrast removes that layer's internal pattern modulation without disabling the corona/wisp itself. Cloud Shimmer controls both base grain and larger pattern modulation: Off leaves smooth density/falloff. Perimeter activity, goal motion, branching and Color Interference remain independent. Playback speed scales all effect time; shimmer drift only changes its own pattern.
- **Color interference** replaces the ambiguous label "seams". **Off / Clustered fringe / Drifting interference** select no effect, localized spectral strands, or moving/mixing spectral strands. Capture and held intensity are independent. It remains edge-only and preserves the existing fill. Nodal structure is an authored approximation, not a rigid-plate simulation.
- **Cloud Corona**, **Flare Corona**, and **Atmosphere Wisps** are separately switchable beneath the corona master. Cloud has its own strength, density and falloff extent; flare has strength and extent for fine curling ridges; atmosphere has strength and wider reach for smoke-like curls. The three-color palette is directly editable. Superposed perimeter waves control localization rather than pulsing the whole circumference together. Preset 1 isolates flare; preset 2 isolates cloud; preset 4 also enables atmosphere.
- The corona now samples the same animated metaball surface and container clipping as the mass. There is no calibrated radius or independent particle ring. Exterior falloff follows closest approach to that surface; a grazing-angle mask restricts the interior tint to a thin fringe. Chromatic separation, tangle density, grain scale and solar-wisp reach control the surface texture.
- Whole goal pulse sends a radial/tangential packet from the aperture through the mass silhouette, independently of boundary packets. Goal pulse amplitude and held motion control the surface deformation. Grid coupling defaults to 0.25, reducing all grid displacement to one quarter while leaving the goal pulse independently adjustable. These are presentation changes; scoring and collision geometry stay unchanged.
- The aperture mask follows the same amplifier displacement as the grid. The independent body pulse tapers to zero at the aperture, avoiding two competing boundary positions. A bounded inverse of the grid map is evaluated once per fragment for Dynamo's top-down orthographic view. Very strong settings that fold the grid over itself cannot have a unique inverse; the intended defaults avoid that regime. Free-perspective camera support and target-device profiling are not certified.
- Capture burst and held charge have separate enable/intensity controls. Capture packets, held travelling wave and occasional held packets are separate switches. The capture burst starts after the absorption delay; packets have an independent dispersive/damped lifetime.
- Wavelength, carrier frequency, Gaussian width, travel speed and dispersion are independent. Along/across ratio and quadrature phase control elliptical/looping displacement. Emission count and spacing create a short train. Playback scales only the presentation clock; it does not rewrite frequency, wavelength, speed or Time.timeScale. Zero playback pauses the treatments; the real gameplay simulation is unaffected.
- Poles: fixed count, separation, orientation/phase, width, alternating activity, optional additional poles per multiplier level. With fixed count, activity strength increases through held intensity. Pole separation is 180° for a pair and is distributed across added poles; extreme values can intentionally overlap regions.
- Branches: density, reach, lifetime and reconnection amount. Separate multiplier-density, multiplier-persistence and multiplier-reach dials enable comparisons. Fine surface ridges and localized wisps replace detached line geometry; drifting noise controls their appearance and recession, with intersecting ridges suggesting reconnection. This is an authored texture, not an electrodynamic topology simulation.
- Grid discharge: independent enable, amplitude, speed, width, wavelength and curl. A continuous spatial displacement is sampled by the existing grid and border shaders at shared coordinates. It preserves line junctions/topology. It is visual deformation, not a force applied to players or the grid physics. High curl can fold lines across themselves.
- Grid rendering fix: each vertex samples the amplifier displacement once. The main grid and border strip use explicit, mutually exclusive draw states, so the border pass cannot redraw the main mesh as an underlying grid. The captured comparison was checked against the earlier duplicated-line frame.

## Implementation and scene context

The physical core's Energy Core and Neutral Shell renderers are enabled in the Dynamo scene. The clip recorder suppresses them with `forceRenderingOff` only inside a single camera render and restores the flags in `finally`; it never changes their serialized enabled state. This prevents exports from leaving a physically active but invisible core. Verified normal/failed export cleanup and a fresh Play Mode spawn with both parts visible.

**Stopping preview:** use **Stop Preview** in the Live Preview window's fixed top/bottom controls, or at the top of the component Inspector. Pause only holds the current frame. Stop clears synthetic capture events, disables the loop, releases the presentation core and reads held multipliers from the real score service. It preserves all layer settings and the selected test multiplier. **Start Preview**, **Capture now**, or enabling **Loop Capture** explicitly restarts visual testing. Entering Play Mode automatically stops preview even if the window is closed or domain/scene reload is disabled. Gameplay capture feedback ignores the preview playback-speed dial; the runtime control panel collapses while preview is off. On returning to edit mode, preview stays stopped.

Handoff validation: pause held the clock; stop cleared preview/pause/loop and released the clone without changing the physical core or layer settings. Entering Play Mode with a looping preview armed stopped it automatically. With spawning completed and scoring opened for an isolated test, placing the real core in goal range and invoking the goal's capture update advanced the actual multiplier from x1 to x2; the treatment read that real held level. These temporary test changes were discarded on exiting Play Mode.

With silhouette smoothing enabled, corona and color interference use the same closest-approach distance as the fill. This removes the dark gap between team 2's white rim and its corona; ray-hit tolerance no longer places the effects on a second, larger contour. The white/black fill and outline colors are unchanged.

The score-void material has **Smooth Silhouette (MSAA)** enabled by default. For Dynamo's top-down orthographic camera and flattened metaballs, it measures closest approach through the balls' narrow depth band using the existing warped/clipped SDF, then converts roughly one pixel of silhouette coverage into MSAA colour/depth coverage. This smooths both the curved outline and moving aperture without a blurred fill or separate contour. The project currently uses 8x MSAA; the clip recorder now inherits the quality setting. This treatment requires an MSAA render target (2x/4x/8x); non-MSAA targets do not receive this smoothing. Perspective/oblique views retain the original hard silhouette. The material checkbox allows comparison. No global quality or scene settings were changed.

Unity 6000.0.28f1, built-in rendering; VectorGridGPU local XY maps to gameplay XZ. The workbench lives on the existing grid object and references both captures, their mass renderers and the core prefab. Reused material property blocks drive the existing mass shader, whose second pass renders surface grain and filaments. Scenes without an enabled treatment retain the original mass shader behavior. The extra surface pass adds rendering cost; a player performance profile has not been run. No packages or physics settings changed.

Source files: AmplifierGoalTreatments.cs owns presentation and preview; AmplifierTreatmentSettings.cs defines live settings and four presets. AmplifierMassSurface.hlsl shares the original mass surface between passes; AmplifierGoalSurface.hlsl supplies deformation and corona. VectorGridNu/AmplifierGridTreatment.hlsl deforms existing grid lines. Editor/AmplifierTreatmentsWindow.cs provides the workbench, AmplifierTreatmentValidation.cs provides repeatable checks, and AmplifierTreatmentRecorder.cs exports deterministic Unity camera frames.

## Theory and MASSIVE-specific choices

Dispersive packets are a good analogy: the envelope spreads, amplitude decreases, and local phase changes. The controls deliberately permit combinations outside one physical dispersion relation so visual contributions can be compared. Color represents authored phase/activity, not a measured spectrum. A physical corona/streamer needs an ionizable medium; these filaments depict latent field activity around the goal. References: [group velocity and envelope motion](https://www.rp-photonics.com/group_velocity.html), [dispersion](https://www.rp-photonics.com/chromatic_dispersion.html), [streamer physics review](https://ir.cwi.nl/pub/30029/).

Suggested MASSIVE reading: keep the goal's black/white identity and banked mass unchanged, use multiplier primarily to increase occupancy/persistence, and reserve broad field displacement for the short capture event. Compare density-only versus reach-only multiplier mappings before combining them. The grid is the natural carrier of the optional discharge because it already represents the playing field's response.
